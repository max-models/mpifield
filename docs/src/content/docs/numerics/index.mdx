mpifield holds fields on the nodes of a uniform 1D, 2D or 3D grid, split over MPI ranks with halo
nodes, and moves values between the nodes and the markers of a particle code: a deposit
(scatter) of marker values onto the nodes and a gather (interpolation) of the field at the
markers. No field equation is solved; the Poisson or Maxwell solver, the pusher and the time
integration belong to the codes that use it.

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: SymPy rules for the shape functions and the operators, run against the
compiled kernels, and the order of convergence of the two gathers, the gradient and the
prescribed field. The pages of the section list the cards module by module; this one shows how
they fit together.

## Deposit and gather

Node $k$ of axis $d$ is at $x_{d,k} = x^{\mathrm{lo}}_d + k h_d$, and a marker at $\mathbf x$ has
the relative coordinates $r_d = (x_d - x^{\mathrm{lo}}_d)/h_d$. A weighting gives node
$\mathbf k$ the weight $W_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf x) = \prod_d w(r_d - k_d)$, a tensor product of
one-dimensional B-splines:

1. **[Linear weights](/mpifield/numerics/kernels-linear-cell/)** (`LINEAR`, cloud-in-cell),
   $w_1$, two nodes per axis: the default.
2. **[Quadratic weights](/mpifield/numerics/kernels-quadratic-cell/)** (`QUADRATIC`,
   triangular-shaped cloud), $w_2$, three nodes per axis around the nearest one: a continuous
   first derivative and less aliasing noise, for $3^n$ nodes per marker instead of $2^n$.

Both sum to one over the nodes and reproduce the position as their first moment, so a deposit
keeps the total value and a gather is exact for constant and linear fields; both gather a
smooth field to second order in $h$. At a bounded edge the weights beyond it fold onto the edge
nodes, which keeps the total; a periodic axis wraps.

The **[deposit and the gather](/mpifield/numerics/weighting/)** are a pair: the gather reads
the nodes and weights the scatter writes, so it is the transpose of the scatter, also at the
edges and the seams. With a symmetric field solver this keeps the momentum: a particle feels no
force of its own.

The deposit is a sum of weights, not a density: divide it by $\prod_d h_d$ for one.

## Over MPI ranks

Each rank stores its block of the grid with halo nodes, at least as many as the weighting needs
(one for `LINEAR`, two for `QUADRATIC`). The kernels see the stored block as a grid of its own
([`Field.block_grid`](/mpifield/numerics/field/)), without the halos beyond a bounded edge of
the domain. A cycle of a particle code then runs, on every rank:

1. select its own markers (from the block's first node up to the next rank's first node);
2. deposit them into the stored block, halo nodes included;
3. `exchange_halos`: add the halos to their owners on the neighbouring ranks;
4. set the field (a solver outside mpifield);
5. `fill_halos`: copy the owners' nodes into the halos;
6. gather the field at its own markers.

The split deposit and gather equal the serial ones.

## Operations on the field

- **[Gradient](/mpifield/numerics/field/)** of a scalar field: central differences, second
  order, one-sided at the ends of a bounded axis.
- **[Smoothing](/mpifield/numerics/field/)**: separable binomial, Gaussian or uniform filters,
  zero outside a bounded axis, wrapped on a periodic one.
- **[Prescribed fields](/mpifield/numerics/prescribed/)**: $\mathbf E = -\nabla\phi$ of a
  given potential, e.g. a table along one axis, for test particles.
- **[Evaluation](/mpifield/numerics/evaluate/)** as xarray: linear interpolation between the
  nodes, whatever the weighting.

## Conventions

Positions are component-major, `(ndim, N)`, and marker values `(ncomp, N)`; a field is
`num_gridpoints + (ncomp,)`. On a bounded axis the $N$ nodes span the domain,
$h = (x^{\mathrm{hi}} - x^{\mathrm{lo}})/(N - 1)$; on a periodic one they cover $N$ cells,
$h = (x^{\mathrm{hi}} - x^{\mathrm{lo}})/N$, and the last node neighbours the first. The compiled
kernels (Pyccel with OpenMP, CUDA, a NumPy fallback, Metal in single precision) share the same
arithmetic.

## Not covered

- No Poisson or Maxwell solver, no particle pusher, no time integration.
- No normalisation of the deposit by the cell volume or by a species weight.
- Only the gradient of a scalar field: no divergence or curl.
- Only linear and quadratic weightings are built in, and no energy-conserving (non-adjoint)
  schemes; a weighting of your own is a pair of kernels.
- Markers are not moved between ranks: each rank deposits and gathers its own, and wraps them on
  a split periodic axis.
- `electric_field_from_potential` differences one-sided at both ends of every axis, also a
  periodic one.

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests compare the Pyccel, CUDA (emulated) and Metal
kernels with the NumPy ones, the deposit with the transpose of the gather, the OpenMP deposit
with the serial one, and, under `mpiexec`, the split deposit, gather, gradient and smoothing
with those of one rank.
